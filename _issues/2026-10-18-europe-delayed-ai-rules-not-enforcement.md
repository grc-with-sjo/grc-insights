## In brief

- The EU moved the AI Act's main "high-risk" duties, for uses such as hiring and credit scoring, to December 2027. Two smaller AI rules still start on 2026-12-02.
- Privacy regulators did not wait: Ireland fined Google EUR 403M over location data, and the Dutch regulator reportedly fined Uber EUR 824.99M over drivers cut off by automated systems ⚠️ verify.
- The UK's new data law is now largely in force, including its rules on automated decisions, and its privacy regulator became the Information Commission on 2026-09-30 ⚠️ verify.

## The story so far

Two EU laws matter most to a North American company with European customers. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is the EU's privacy law, enforced by national regulators who coordinate through the European Data Protection Board (EDPB). The AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is the EU's risk-based AI law, and its heaviest duties fall on "high-risk" systems. Those duties were due on 2026-08-02 ⚠️ verify.

In July the EU amended the AI Act through the AI Omnibus and pushed those dates out. A second package, the Digital Omnibus, proposes to loosen parts of the GDPR, and it is still being negotiated. The UK runs its own version of the GDPR and has been phasing in reforms through the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA).

This issue argues that Europe delayed its new AI rules, not its enforcement of the old ones, and that your plan should follow the enforcement.

## What happened

### The AI Act's big deadline moved to 2027, but December 2026 still bites

Start with the delay, because it is real but narrow. The AI Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) amends the AI Act. In force since 2026-07-27, it moves the duties for stand-alone high-risk systems, listed in the Act's Annex III, to 2027-12-02, and those for AI built into regulated products (Annex I) to 2028-08-02. [Source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202601744)

Two things did not move. From 2026-12-02, generative AI systems placed on the market before 2026-08-02 must meet the Act's rule on marking AI-generated output (Article 50(2)), and new prohibitions apply, described by secondary sources as covering non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material ⚠️ verify.

Evidence on general-purpose AI models, the large models other products build on, is thin. Secondary reporting says the European Commission's powers over their providers, including fines up to the higher of EUR 15M or 3% of worldwide turnover, became usable on 2026-08-02 ⚠️ verify. [WSGR](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2026/08/eu-ai-act-enforcement-phase-begins/) The research found no action, but could not read the Commission's page or check guidelines, standards or national authorities.

The delay buys time for hiring and credit models. It buys none for generative AI features.

**Takeaway:** Keep high-risk inventory work going, but put generative AI marking and the new prohibitions first for 2026-12-02.

**Open question:** When the Commission first uses its powers over general-purpose models, will it start with a fine or with more dialogue?

### Privacy regulators fined automated decisions and location tracking

While the AI deadlines moved, GDPR regulators used the law they already have. Ireland's Data Protection Commission (DPC), the national GDPR regulator, fined Google EUR 403M on 2026-09-21. It covered processing under three location and activity settings from 2018-05-25 to 2020-02-04. The DPC found failures on lawfulness, transparency, retention and accountability, and gave Google six months to comply. [Source](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/data-protection-commission-fines-google-eu403-million-following-inquiry-googles-processing-location)

The Dutch regulator, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (AP), fined Uber EUR 824.99M, according to Dutch press ⚠️ verify. The case concerns driver accounts deactivated solely by automated means, and transparency. Uber says it will appeal ⚠️ verify. [ioplus](https://www.ioplus.nl/nl/posts/uber-beboet-met-825-miljoen--wat-het-echt-betekent) Article 22 of the GDPR, in plain words, limits decisions made by a system alone when they significantly affect a person. The human involvement has to be real, not a rubber stamp.

GDPR relief is not close. Germany and France had the member states' Digital Omnibus vote in Coreper, the committee of their EU ambassadors, postponed from 2026-10-07 to 2026-10-11. [Agence Europe](https://agenceurope.eu/en/bulletin/article/13954/5/vote-on-digital-omnibus-postponed-until-11-october-under-pressure-from-paris-and-berlin) The European Parliament's draft drew more than 1,750 amendments ⚠️ verify. [Acompli](https://acompli.ie/news/digital-omnibus-gdpr-cookies-status-september-2026/) With no Parliament position, no GDPR change will apply in 2026.

**Takeaway:** Put automated decisions that cut people off, such as deactivations, fraud flags and credit refusals, on your risk register with evidence of human review.

**Open question:** Where does a human "in the loop" end and a human who only confirms the system's answer begin?

### The UK's automated-decision rules are already live

The UK shows the same pattern. The DUAA reforms UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018 and the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR), the UK's cookie and e-marketing rules. Most of its data-protection part, including the new automated-decision rules, came into force on 2026-02-05. From 2026-06-19, organisations must acknowledge a data-protection complaint within 30 days and investigate it. [Source](https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82)

On 2026-09-30 the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), the UK privacy regulator, became the Information Commission, run by a board, with existing investigations continuing ⚠️ verify. [Recordinglaw](https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/uk-information-commission-transfer-september-2026/) Complaints to the ICO rose 81% to 76,743 in 2025-26, and 27.4% were answered within 90 days ⚠️ verify. [Freevacy](https://www.freevacy.com/news/ico/ico-publishes-2025-26-annual-report/7587)

The rules apply now. With the regulator this stretched, the complaints duty puts first-line handling on you.

**Takeaway:** Treat the 30-day complaint acknowledgement as a live service level, run through one tracked queue.

**Open question:** Will a board-led Information Commission enforce more, or less, than a single commissioner did?

> **This week:** Research for 2026-10-04 to 2026-10-11 is incomplete, so treat the week as unknown, not quiet. The EDPB posted the Uber fine and a Greek breach decision on 2026-10-08 ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news_en)). The Coreper vote, moved to Sunday 2026-10-11 ⚠️ verify, has no known outcome ([Agence Europe](https://agenceurope.eu/en/bulletin/article/13954/5/vote-on-digital-omnibus-postponed-until-11-october-under-pressure-from-paris-and-berlin)). Nothing was found on the AI Act or the UK, and regulator sites were not checked directly.

## Where I land

Plan to the enforcement, not the delay. The AI Omnibus gave high-risk systems more time, and I would use it, not bank it. But it changed nothing about the GDPR, which is where the fines landed.

The two fines that stood out were about things many SaaS, banking and retail firms do every day: tracking location, and letting a system decide who keeps an account. Neither needed a new law. Neither was about AI training data either, and the research found no confirmed fine on that, so I would not let model-training worries crowd out the basics. The UK's automated-decision rules have applied since February.

So this quarter I would put three things ahead of Annex III readiness: an automated-decision register with the human reviewer named, a generative AI inventory checked against 2026-12-02, and a UK complaint process that meets 30 days.

I cannot confirm a single action on general-purpose AI models. I read that as "not yet visible", not "not coming".

The trade-off I accept: spending on old-law controls while high-risk AI preparation slows. If the Commission moves fast, I will be behind on AI documentation. I would rather be behind on a deadline that moved than on a fine that already happened.

## The questions still open

- Whether the Digital Omnibus loosens the GDPR at all: Germany and France are pressing on trade secrets and a "low-risk controller" category.
- What counts as real human review: banks and retailers run automated decisions at a scale where reviewing each one is hard to evidence.
- When general-purpose AI enforcement becomes visible: no action was found, and the Commission's own pages went unread.

## What to do this quarter

- List generative AI features offered in the EU before 2026-08-02, confirm output marking by 2026-12-02, and check them against the new prohibitions ⚠️ verify.
- Build an automated-decision register for EU and UK users: decision, effect, human reviewer, evidence.
- Review location and activity data for lawful basis, transparency and retention, using the Google findings as a checklist.
- If you anonymise data or scrape the web for AI, comment on the EDPB's July draft guidelines by 2026-10-30. [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-sheds-light-on-anonymisation-and-web-scraping-for-generative-ai-and-adopts-final-version_en)
