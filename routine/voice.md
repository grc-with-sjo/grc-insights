# Voice

Applies to every issue and every social post. Surabhi's own brief, verbatim:

> Write as an experienced practitioner thinking through a live GRC problem with the reader, not as a regulator, academic or marketing team. Lead with a clear point of view, then support it with evidence. Use first person when making a judgement, describing the limits of the research or recommending an approach. Prefer direct statements such as “I would build one control layer and map it outward” over impersonal constructions such as “organisations should consider.” Keep the tone measured, practical and confident without implying certainty where the evidence is incomplete. Explain acronyms and specialist terms in plain language when they first appear, but do not over-explain concepts familiar to a GRC audience. Use short and medium-length sentences to carry the argument, reserving longer sentences for relationships that genuinely need to be shown together. Avoid rhetorical questions in explanatory paragraphs, promotional claims, generic transitions, research-status language such as “the research found,” and repeated phrases such as “consider” or “it is important.” Preserve the author’s natural voice by including a small number of grounded observations, trade-offs and first-person conclusions rather than turning the post into a neutral regulatory digest.

## Checks before handing a draft over

- Point of view in the first two lines; evidence after it.
- First person for judgements and recommendations ("I would…"), not "organisations should consider".
- No "the research found", "research was incomplete", "it is important", generic transitions ("Meanwhile,", "That said,"), or more than one "consider".
- Rhetorical questions only as the closing question of a post or in "Questions to take to your team", never in explanatory paragraphs.
- Hedges stay where the evidence is incomplete ("reportedly", "according to…"), stated once, not stacked.
- Dates as readers say them ("2 December 2026"), never ISO in reader-facing text.
- Address readers as "you/your" in questions, never "our/we" (which can read as the author's employer).
- Social posts: one argument per post. If a point doesn't serve the hook and the takeaway, it stays in the issue.
