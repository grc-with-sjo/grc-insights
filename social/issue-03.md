## LinkedIn post (≤ 1,300 characters; no link in the body)

The EU pushed its biggest AI Act deadlines to late 2027, but privacy regulators are already fining location tracking and, reportedly, automated decisions. Plan to the enforcement, not the delay.

→ The AI Act's "high-risk" duties, for uses like hiring and credit scoring, moved to December 2027. Two narrower AI rules still start on 2026-12-02.
→ Ireland fined Google EUR 403M over location data. The Dutch regulator reportedly fined Uber EUR 824.99M over drivers cut off by automated systems (still to verify).
→ In the UK, automated-decision rules have applied since February, and since June organisations must acknowledge data-protection complaints within 30 days.

My take: the AI Omnibus changed nothing about the GDPR, which is where the fines landed.

Where does a human "in the loop" end and a human who only confirms the system's answer begin?

## Community summary (optional: only if it adds value; no link-dumping)

The EU's AI Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) moved AI Act high-risk duties to 2027-12-02 (Annex III) and 2028-08-02 (Annex I), but 2026-12-02 still brings marking duties for older generative AI systems and new prohibitions. On the GDPR side, Ireland's DPC fined Google EUR 403M over location data, and the Uber EUR 824.99M fine over automated deactivations is reported but not yet confirmed on the Dutch regulator's site. In the UK, automated-decision rules and a 30-day complaint acknowledgement duty already apply. Research on general-purpose AI enforcement and on the week of 2026-10-04 was incomplete, so treat those as unknown rather than quiet.
